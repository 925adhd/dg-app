How this is calculated
  1. Start from each item's sale price — this is the price you'll actually be charged before any coupons, so fold in any "Instant Savings" markdown shown on the shelf tag here too.
  2. Apply any multi-buy deal (Buy X Get Y free/%/$ off, X for $ bundle pricing, or Buy X Save $) based on how many you're buying. Mark what it "counts as" — a store offer, a DG coupon, or a manufacturer coupon — since that decides when it applies, whether it counts toward the basket-coupon threshold, and whether it counts toward the tax you owe. A deal marked as a DG coupon or manufacturer coupon locks the matching field below it, since only one of each is allowed per item.
  3. Check each spend & save deal ("Save $5 when you spend $25") against the items you ticked for it, using their price after sale prices and store offer deals. A "buy any X items" deal ("Save $2 on any 2") counts how many of the ticked items you're buying instead — mixing different products is fine. If you spend enough, the savings are spread proportionally across those items — and if the deal repeats, you get it again for every full threshold you reach (spend $30 on a "$5 off $15" deal and you save $10). A deal counted as Instant Savings comes off right away, one counted as a DG coupon comes off with the DG coupons, and one counted as a manufacturer coupon comes off at the very end, after tax is figured.
  4. Subtract DG store/digital coupons (and any multi-buy deal marked as one) — a coupon never pushes an item below $0.
  5. Check your subtotal against the basket coupon's threshold. Manufacturer coupons — including multi-buy deals marked as manufacturer coupons — don't count toward this check.
  6. If you qualify, the basket coupon (like $5 off $25) is spread proportionally across your qualifying items, the same way Dollar General's registers do it.
  7. Sales tax is calculated here — on the price after sale price, DG coupons, and the basket coupon, but before manufacturer coupons. A manufacturer coupon is reimbursed to the store by the manufacturer rather than being a store price cut, so most states still tax you on the pre-coupon price.
  8. Manufacturer coupons come off last, each capped at whatever the item still costs — no overage. They lower what you pay, but not what you were just taxed on.

Not affiliated with or endorsed by Dollar General. Tax rates are estimates — actual store totals can vary by a few cents depending on local tax rules and rounding.

Google Sheet sync Not connected

Keep your purchases in a Google Sheet you own, so they're backed up and the same on every device.

  1. Make a new Google Sheet.
  2. In the sheet, open Extensions → Apps Script.
  3. Delete the code that's there and paste in the sync script. or open it here.
  4. Tap . Then in Apps Script go to Project Settings (gear) → Script Properties → Add script property, name it SECRET and paste the key as its value.
  5. Click Deploy → New deployment → Web app. Set Execute as: Me and Who has access: Anyone, then deploy and copy the web app URL.
  6. Paste the URL and key below and tap Connect.

The URL and key are saved only in this browser, never on the website or in its code. Anyone who has both can read and change the sheet, so keep them private.

Changes save to your sheet automatically a moment after you make them. When you open the app, it loads the latest from the sheet.
